# Paid Time Off (PTO) Policy
Document ID: POL-PTO-2024-02
Effective Date: March 1, 2024
Owner: People Operations
Last Reviewed: September 1, 2024

## 1. Purpose
This policy explains how employees accrue, request, and use paid time off, including carryover limits, blackout periods, and payout rules at separation. PTO is intended to support rest, personal obligations, and well-being. Nothing in this policy limits legally protected leave. Where local law provides greater benefits, the law controls.

## 2. Eligibility
All full-time employees begin accruing PTO on their start date. Part-time employees scheduled for twenty (20) or more hours per week accrue PTO on a pro-rated basis relative to a forty-hour week. Interns and temporary workers do not accrue PTO unless stated in writing in an offer letter or contract. Employees moving from ineligible to eligible status begin accruing on the effective date of the status change; prior periods are not retroactively credited.

## 3. Accrual Rates
Full-time employees with less than three (3) years of service accrue one and one-half (1.5) days of PTO per month, which equals eighteen (18) days per year. Employees with three (3) or more years of continuous service accrue two (2) days per month, which equals twenty-four (24) days per year. Accrual is posted at the end of each pay period. Accrual pauses during unpaid leaves longer than thirty (30) calendar days and resumes when the employee returns to paid status. Negative balances are not permitted except with People Operations approval for emergencies, and any advance must be repaid from future accruals or final pay where lawful.

## 4. Requesting Time Off
PTO requests should be submitted in the HR system at least five (5) business days in advance for absences of three (3) or more consecutive days. Managers should approve or deny requests within three (3) business days and must not unreasonably withhold approval when coverage exists. Same-day sick leave should be reported before the start of the shift when possible, with the formal request entered by the next business day. Employees should ensure critical handoffs are documented before longer vacations. Departments may require a coverage plan for absences of five (5) or more consecutive business days.

## 5. Carryover
Unused PTO may carry over into the next calendar year up to a maximum of five (5) days. Accrued balances above the carryover cap are forfeited on December 31 unless local law requires payout or continued accrual. Employees are encouraged to schedule time off evenly through the year rather than banking large balances. People Operations publishes remaining balances in the HR system each quarter.

## 6. Blackout Periods
Product launch weeks and the last two (2) weeks of the fiscal year may be designated blackout periods by department leadership with at least thirty (30) days’ notice. PTO during blackout periods is granted only for emergencies, illness, or legally protected leave. Already-approved PTO that later falls into a newly announced blackout period will be honored unless a documented business emergency requires rescheduling, in which case managers must work with the employee in good faith.

## 7. Holidays and Other Leave
Company holidays are separate from PTO and are published annually. If a holiday falls during an approved PTO period, the holiday is not deducted from the PTO balance. Unpaid leave, parental leave, and medical leave are covered in separate policies. Employees should contact People Operations early when planning extended leave so benefits and accrual impacts can be explained.

## 8. Separation
Upon voluntary resignation with at least two (2) weeks’ notice, unused accrued PTO up to the carryover cap is paid out where required by law or company practice. Employees terminated for cause are not eligible for PTO payout except where mandated by applicable law. The company may deduct advanced but unearned PTO from final pay only where permitted.

## 9. Questions
Questions about balances, accrual, or exceptions should be sent to people@example.com. Managers with questions about coverage or blackout designations should consult People Operations before denying a request.
